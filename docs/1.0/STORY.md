# firstlight ... the story

story architect, 2026-11-13. the spine is `SAGA.md` (14 beats, 5 acts, the gates, the api) and stays as built. this is
what happens inside it. every line is in `src/story-script.js`; every branch is specified in `BRANCHES.md`.

## the premise

the roads between the worlds are going dark, and a patient man in a grey coat is replacing them with roads that never
break. nothing is ever lost on his roads. nothing ever leaves them either. you are someone who mends things in a small
valley, and an envoy of heaven came to you expecting a fortress and found a person who knows how to begin. then his
road takes your fox, and he sends a receipt.

## what it is about

- **keeping is not the same as being with.** his roads keep what crosses them. a true road lets go at both ends: you
  may leave, and you may come back. the price of an open road is that things can be lost on it. the game asks whether
  you can love something you are not allowed to keep.
- **he has a point.** roads do break. the river does rise. the warning did fail, and heaven was late, twice. nobody
  on his roads is hurt; they sign, and most of them thank him. everything he says is true. that is why he is
  frightening.
- **no master key.** the cure is not a better keeper. it is many hands, each holding one anchor, each able to be
  tired, or late, or wrong. "then who counts them." "everyone. badly. together."
- **it rises from below.** the prophecy is read at the spring, reread in heaven (from the ones who were taken), and
  shown a third time in the finale, when the taken come down every road into the valley.

## the one invention that ties it together

the regent of ash was a bell keeper named **hollis wick**, at the crossing that is now bellweather, about a hundred
years ago. the river rose. he rang the bell, as he was meant to. forty-one people ran to the sound, onto the bridge,
and the bridge was the thing that was failing. the warning from heaven reached him the morning after. the one who
carried it was **the envoy**: she was held at a gate over a point of ceremony, and she read him the message anyway,
because that was the office.

he walked to the harbour city bellglass, half drowned, and was let in as a friend. he sat all night with the
roadkeepers' die (the stamp every true road is struck from), filed one stroke of it shut, left his list of forty-one
names on the table, and took the die down to the only place that would lease him a foundry. since then he has not
lost one traveller. below they call him regent: the one who minds a thing until its owner comes back. nobody has come
back. ash is what is left when the fire has finished taking.

this makes the title of act iii true three ways. the night the warning failed is his night. it is your night, at the
same bridge, in the same kind of flood, with the bell you repaired. and both times the warning was hers, and late.

the envoy has known who he is since he showed himself at the beacon. she does not tell you until a drowned daybook
makes her. that is her arc: from an officer who delivers messages and leaves, to someone who stays, says the true
thing to a face, and steps onto the road.

all of this is a proposal inside the canon's frame (a lieutenant of hell who builds a counterfeit road network). it
changes no built beat.

## the cast

each: want · wound · voice · two lines from the script. only three people in the game never use a contraction: the
envoy, the regent and neris. everyone else talks like the valley.

**the envoy.** wants to deliver one warning in time. wound: bellweather. voice: no contractions, exact, lists of
three and then a turn; her jokes arrive a beat late and dry.
"I was told I would find a fortress. An army. Someone prepared. Instead, I found someone who knows how to begin."
"I have made tea. I understand mortals take it hot. I have never seen the appeal of waiting for a drink to stop
hurting you."

**the regent of ash** (a smiling stranger; hollis wick). wants to stop counting heads at dusk and coming up short.
wound: forty-one. voice: no contractions, gentle, a clerk's nouns (received, kept, owed, noted). he never raises his
voice and never lies.
"The warning did not fail because it came late. It worked. That is the part I could not carry."
"You have heard me out. So you know I mean it. I wish I did not."

**briar.** a briarfox with a barb in her leg who decided, once, to come along. no lines. never at heel: a little
wide, a little ahead, waiting where she has decided you are going, put upon. she steals gloves, sneezes at holy
things, hears bells before they ring, and will go ahead of you anywhere except onto a bridge.
"Not at heel. A little wide, a little ahead, stopping to disapprove of things."
"She does not check whether you are following. She never did."

**oren.** builder. wants things to hold. wound: he can mend anything that is an object. voice: contractions, joints
and glue, feeling hidden in a repair.
"Bring back what you can. And bring back yourself. I can remake the rest."
"I made her a second one. For the workshop. Pear wood. I hadn't told you yet."

**mara.** researcher. wants to know. voice: quick, contracted, labels everything, treats "I don't know" as a result.
"I don't know what it means. That's a real answer. Write it down."
"A null result is still a result."

**ilan.** their grown child, a musician with an unfinished last song. voice: light, self-mocking.
"I tried to play something. There's nothing in the guitar. I checked."
"It's got a rest in it now, about a fox long. I'm keeping the rest."

**tessa.** road merchant. wants a road that stops eating her people. wound: two drivers buried. voice: brisk, counts
in coin and miles, jokes first.
"If you're bandits, I should tell you the cart's broken, the stock's wet and I'm in a mood."
"You've known me for one cart repair. Tell me anyway. Would you sign?"

**rowan.** bell keeper of bellweather. wants a voice to call the lost home. wound: he inherited a silent bell and
nobody would tell him why. voice: slow, clipped, country; the bell is she.
"The road is open. Now we need a voice to call the lost home."
"I rang her and they came to the sound, the way they're meant to. Straight to the bridge. I keep turning that over."

**fenna.** carrier on the mill road. says the least of anyone and brings what is needed.
"I heard. I brought bread."
"Cart's outside. Somebody'll want carrying after."

**neris.** kept the foundry's books until she could not. wants the record whole, with her own part in it. voice: no
contractions, flat, exact, harder on herself than anyone will be.
"I was good at it. I want that written down as well. I could make a missing person look like a rounding error."
"I chose the forty. I have chosen who counts before. It felt exactly the same."

**veyr.** warden of the foundry gate, for the landlords. voice: cold aphorisms.
"An escape is a restraint I have not finished."
"You put my herald's last link in a jar. I have had worse wardens than a jar."

**rielle.** heaven's bellwright. wants instruments that answer tired hands. voice: a craftsman's warmth.
"A public instrument must answer someone with tired hands as readily as a practiced musician."
"There. A gate with no opinion. I've built finer things. I haven't built many better."

**damar.** a carrier stranded in bellglass. formal, patient, aggrieved in full sentences.
"Sixteen days I have waited in this bay, with a load addressed to two places. One address is false."
"I will not tell you it was wrong. I will stand a little further off."

**anik.** keeps the lamps lit at the edge of the stars. soft, notices who did the work.
"Somebody kept the lamps lit. That was me. I only say so because nobody else will."
"We can hold this without you. That's rather the point."

**avar.** the scholar who built the still wheel: one setting for every branch. a small regent, and he learns.
"It was the only arrangement in which nothing fell. I checked. I am still right about that."
"Yes. I see it. Allow me a moment to resent it."

**vessa.** bridge keeper of the coast. lights the pier.
"That's all a lantern is for."
"A bridge belongs to its crossings, not to whoever can hold a chain across it."

also speaking: istra and tovan (hell), calen, yselle and the bell courier (heaven), sahra and ilyra (bellglass), and
the drowned witness (a daybook read aloud).

## act i · the valley

**kit** (`kit.workshop`). oren hands you a blade, a lantern and three tonics, and a joke about each. the turn is his
last line: bring back yourself. feeling: you are cared for by someone who shows it with objects.

**hart** (`hart.descent`, `hart.briar`, `hart.fallen`, `hart.home`). mara shows you a mark cut into every seventh
step of the stair under the wood: a small road with both ends open. she doesn't know what it means, and says so. in
an alcove a fox lets you treat her leg and then simply comes along, not at heel. the hollow hart falls like something
finally allowed to, and where its heart should be is the same mark. a white feather lies on the dais; the fox sneezes
at it. at home she is holding oren's good glove, and he goes to find a bowl. the turn: the valley has made room for
her before you have named her.

**envoy** (`envoy.arrival`, `envoy.prophecy`). she stands in the spring without disturbing it, and the fox goes
straight to her. her first lines are kept word for word. new: she has never before had to revise her opening. she
speaks the prophecy as something she was taught as a comfort and now suspects is an instruction. the turn: "it was
not asking me." her line that hell is breaking the roads is what she believes; hell and heaven each correct it later.

**feather** (`feather.sight`, `feather.gift`). hold it up like a question, not a torch. the valley's roads light
gold, including the hair-thin track the fox has worn to the door. she teaches the rule you use to the end: a true
road lets go at both ends. the valley has no red road. the world beyond has more every season.

## act ii · the road

**road** (`road.gate`, `road.tessa`, `road.mended`, `road.spot`, `road.ram`). the fox stops dead at the stone bridge:
the one place she will not go ahead. tessa is funny before she is sad: two drivers buried on this road. somewhere in
here the fox claims a spot (the place you stop most) and is found lying in it as if you were late. the ram falls and
is all scar and no malice.

**beacon** (`beacon.stranger`, `.answer`, `beacon.tessa`, `beacon.door`, `beacon.herald`, `beacon.cinder`).
*regent sighting 1.* before the breaches, a tall man in a grey coat has already done tessa's sums and is sorry about
them. a road that never breaks, for one signature. asked the cost: "nothing you would miss." you may hear him out (he
kept a crossing once; he no longer comes up short) or cut him off (he writes something down). the feather shows the
spur he climbed burning ash red: the first counterfeit road you see. tessa asks what you would do (**choice 1**). he
leaves a gift that is also a hook: the herald will drop a small warm thing, keep it, nothing is owed. the beacon is a
door; the family holds it, each in their own trade, and the fox has oren's other glove. the herald's last link sits in
the ash, patient as a cat (**choice 2**). the turn: the first villain you meet is the kindest person on the ridge.

**bellweather** (`bellweather.rowan`, `.clapper`, `.keeper`, `.bell`). rowan oils a bell that has not rung since
before his grandfather kept her. the feather, raised out of habit, shows a faint red road through the square to the
bridge, old as the flagstones; nobody here has ever not walked on it. the fox finds the clapper wrapped in a child's
coat: somebody small hid it so the bell could never call anyone again. under the hushbound keeper, scratched into the
flagstone: forty-one short lines. the fox's ears go up a breath before the first note. the last keeper rang her once,
in a flood, and never again. the turn: you have done a good thing whose history you do not know.

## act iii · the night the warning failed

**night** (`night.fall`, `night.found`, `night.across`, `night.bridge`, `night.receipt`, `night.envoy`, `night.quiet`,
`night.device`, `night.device.after`).

the sky goes red like a lid coming down, and the river is over the lower piles. rowan rings them in, and five
travellers come to the sound, to the bridge, exactly as they are meant to. the old red road through the square is
bright now and laying itself over the bridge plank by plank. the fox, who has never crossed a bridge ahead of you, is
on this one before you can stop her, standing on something in the middle of the span, and will not come back.

you get all five across. the old bridge holds. this time; tessa felt it think about it. that is his point, shown: the
honest road only happened to hold (and if you told tessa to tear it up, her second wagon went in at the ford). rowan
stops ringing, and in the quiet you can hear what the fox is standing on. it is ticking.

a road has two ends. this is a thing for tying one of them shut. she looks at you once, the way she does when she has
decided where you are both going, and bites through the seam. the red goes out of the bridge like water out of a
cloth, and it takes her with it. no sound. a place on the planks the shape of a fox, and then not.

where the device was bolted, the ash has settled into handwriting: *received in good order: one fox, answering to
briar. kept safe. nothing owed.* he believes it. that is the punch after the punch.

the envoy could not step onto the road to help. she came to warn you what was on the bridge, and reached the spring
as the sky turned. she was late, and says so. she does not bargain, slowly, because someone else will say the opposite
very kindly. a road goes somewhere; she will help find where; his roads are stamped from one stolen pattern in a
foundry under the world. not tonight. go home.

then the quiet: no marker. the bowl by the door, which someone has filled, can be visited as long as she is gone.
oren is holding a second bowl he had not told you about and cannot find a place to put it down. mara will not say it
will be all right. ilan checked the guitar and it is empty. fenna brought bread.

in the morning the device is only iron with tooth marks in it (**choice 3**). rowan cannot stop thinking that he rang
and they came.

## act iv · the stolen pattern

**hell** (`hell.arrival`, `hell.neris`, `hell.tovan`, `hell.gate`, `hell.veyr`, `hell.veyr-down`, `hell.pattern`,
`hell.regent`, `hell.choice`, `hell.after`). hell is not on fire. it is at work. istra corrects the envoy: hell is a
place, he is a man, and they would like their name back. every yard of his road is held down by a name: a person's
own way home, signed over and hammered flat. nobody is beaten. they are fed and warm and cannot leave, and most of
them thanked him. veyr holds the gate for the landlords. neris kept the books and logged the engine that took your
fox. the feather finds the one gold mark among a thousand red: the master die, the stair mark from home with one
stroke filed shut. veyr falls reaching for his book, and four hundred people look up and do not run, because nobody
has told them the door is a door.

*regent sighting 2.* behind the works he has been waiting for the noise to stop. the first thing he gives you is
that she is safe, fed, warm, asleep. hear him and you get the bell, the bridge and the forty-one, and the line the
story hangs on: it worked. and while you stand listening, a clerk slides a grey paper to a woman with a child on her
hip, and she signs. then he is curious what you will do about the line (**choice 4**). cutting the writ frees all of
them and drops the road dark under whoever is standing on it tonight. taking forty out strands nobody and is choosing
who counts. signing them to the ninth office is shorter shifts, better bread, and names on a road. there is no clean
hand here, and neris will not let you pretend.

**heaven** (`heaven.arrival`, `heaven.garden`, `heaven.home`, `heaven.reading`, `heaven.turn`, `heaven.seraph`,
`heaven.choice`, `heaven.after`). seen before it is defended. nobody stops you or asks what you are for. the first
hour is for sitting. a gardener keeps beans planted whatever happens at the walls. the people you freed are at the
garden gate asking politely whether this is real. you keep looking for the fox at the edge of things.

the envoy's house is two rooms, a chair that has clearly just been carried in for you, and a shelf of hundreds of
sealed letters: her copies of the warnings that came too late. that shelf is heaven's number. if you heard him in the
foundry she says the first true thing about it: you let him speak; she did not, once; she read him her message and
left. rielle reads the die: heaven's own stamp, one stroke filed shut. the envoy rereads the prophecy and admits she
had it wrong twice: *from the ones who were taken, not from the deep.*

then every bell changes key. the rescue corridors, the envoy's own roads, answer to him now; everyone heaven sent out
to bring people home is walking back in on his road. this is what "heaven is under attack" meant: every road still
standing, and none of them theirs. she cannot set foot on them. you can. the relay seraph is a mirror in the shape of
an angel; the courier it nearly kept says it was not cruel, only so even underfoot. afterward: how should the garden
gate welcome people, with four hundred standing at it (**choice 5**)? the envoy will not advise: she once lost a
morning at a gate like this one.

**witness** (the sea and the stars, in either order). `witness.harbour`, `witness.guardian`, `witness.record`,
`witness.choice`, `witness.sea.after`: bellglass keeps its records in the one room the sea was never allowed. the
guardian was guarding a shelf. the daybook's last entry gives him a name and a list, and the envoy, who has read
ahead of you and is standing very straight, says: "I was the warning." she has known who he is since the beacon. she
is telling you now. (**choice 6a**: the wall, the few who need it, or his permit, which would open every one of his
roads to you, including the one your fox is on.)

`witness.stars`, `witness.meridian`, `witness.avar`, `witness.stars.after`, `witness.confluence`, `witness.regent`,
`witness.trail`: past the last lamp, avar's still wheel keeps every branch of the sky in step and starves one of them
on the hour so the rest run smooth. you stop it, and for one breath nothing keeps time. then the sky is on the
anchors you set by hand. you are standing at one; raven is at the other, round the curve; anik tells you not to go
and check hers. it shakes, which it never did, and it holds. this is the cure, and it is worse, and avar needs a
moment to resent it. (**choice 6b**: let the marks speak, or let avar say his piece.)

*regent sighting 3.* he is sitting beside the knot all his roads run to, and here he names himself: regent, the one
who minds a thing until its owner comes back; ash, what cannot be burned twice. a hundred keepers, a hundred keys,
every one a person who can be tired, or late, or wrong: you are building a world out of his worst night and calling
it a cure. he answers what you did in the harbour and with avar. hear him a third time and he stops smiling. he
tells you where she is himself, because he wants you to see how well she has been kept.

**nearshore** (`nearshore.pier`, `nearshore.offer`, `nearshore.heel`, `nearshore.false`, `nearshore.real`,
`nearshore.home`). vessa has lit every lantern. the envoy can tell a soul from an echo but not which one is yours.
two foxes on the boards, the same scar, the same ear. one comes to your left heel at once and sits, perfectly. the
feather shows both gold: he has learned. his offer is the theme said plainly: the one at your heel will never run
ahead, never be anywhere you cannot find her; the other will leave you again one day; which can you live with? the
second fox trots off toward the place you actually stopped most, and does not check whether you follow. she never
did. every habit she showed you since the mine is the answer, and the perfect one has none of them.

tell the quiet one to come home (you have to say it) and she is the right warmth and does nothing wrong, and that is
how you know, and she comes apart like ash on water; he writes it down. go to the real one and she sniffs your hand,
sneezes, and leans too hard, and back on the pier vessa laughs out loud, once. that evening she inventories the house
and lies down by her bowl. ilan found the last song.

## act v · firstlight

**finale** (`finale.eve`, `finale.rise`, `finale.chain.*`, `finale.allies`, `finale.ally.*`, `finale.runs`,
`finale.merged.*`, `finale.offer`, `finale.answer`, `finale.last`, `finale.ending`). he will not come to burn the
valley. he will come to keep it.

*phase 1.* he stands in the stopped spring with the book, and reads your own decisions back as chains: the forty you
chose, the permit you took, the engine in your pack, the fox you told to come home. he is fair about it, in his way.
then people come down every road into the valley, soot on some, salt on some, one with a toolbag, and the envoy says
the second reading aloud: from the ones who were taken. look at them. each arrives in their own voice. if you gave
him nothing, he hangs his own chain, the oldest he has.

*phase 2.* he runs, and the valley folds into every world at once to let him. the envoy steps down off the water
onto the dirt for the first time and opens her wings to stand in the way: she has been late to a great many things,
not this. the fox runs ahead on a trail no feather can show, and is sitting on his hidden road when he gets there,
put upon, as if he were late.

*phase 3.* on one knee, he gives you your breath back and makes the offer he made tessa: one signature, a life that
cannot be hurt, for you, the fox and everyone in the valley. if you told tessa to sign, he only asks you to take
your own advice. the envoy: he is not lying. she checked. (**choice 7**.)

**the endings.**
- *the open road.* refuse, bleed for every yard, and break the book. the pages rise as gold roads that let go at both
  ends. he tells you they will all get lost, and he is right that they can. the camera lifts to everything you lit.
- *the first answer.* only if you heard him all three times. at the last blow you pick the book up and read him his
  first page, forty-one names, slowly, badly. the envoy tells him to his face that she was late and it was not his
  bell. nobody forgives him; nobody was asked to. "then who counts them." "everyone. badly. together." he closes the
  book without marking his place. what is left in the spring is a bell keeper getting his boots wet. he spends the
  epilogue on the bellweather bridge reading every name aloud each dusk.
- *the kept valley.* sign. the shaking stops, and you can no longer feel the fox against your leg. he does not die
  and you do not take his place: he closes the book on your name, looking like a man whose count came out right.
  *received in good order: one traveller, one fox, one valley. kept safe. nothing owed.* the valley is perfect and
  still. the envoy does not bargain for you; she comes to the edge of his road every evening and says your name. the
  fox is not by the door. she is out past the last lantern, waiting, the way she waits anywhere she has decided you
  are going.

**epilogue** (`epilogue.workshop`, `.spring`, `.bridge`, `.lookout`, `.observatory`, `.pier`, `.garden`, `.concert`,
`.credits`). walk the valley. oren has mended the new arch twice already and is glad: a thing you never have to mend
isn't yours. the windbells on it are out of tune and he is leaving them. the fox chose the pear wood bowl. tessa has
a wagon that pulls to the left, or a company where she loses most of the votes. the engine hangs under the bell, or
is forty pages in mara's hand (it is a hinge), or is buried under the arch. a page is nailed to the pier post. a
white stair still comes down to the garden. the envoy gives you her name, because envoys only have names for the
people they come back to. ilan plays the last song, and in the rest that is exactly a fox long, the fox sneezes. in
the kept valley he plays it without a mistake. "huh. that's how it was written."

## the branches in one look

1. **tessa's waybill** (beacon): i would tear it up · your road, your call · i would sign.
2. **the cinder** (beacon): keep · seal · later, put it down.
3. **the quiet engine** (night): under the bell in the open · two locks in the observatory · carried.
4. **the foundry's people** (hell): cut the writ · take the forty · sign them over.
5. **the garden's welcome** (heaven): a keeper who asks · a gate with no opinion.
6. **the witness** (sea and stars): his name on a wall, kept for the few, or beside your permit · the marks alone, or
   with avar's piece.
7. **the two foxes and the last answer**: which fox you tell to come home; refuse or sign; end it, or read him his
   first page.

running through all of it: whether you heard him out, three times.

none of these is a goodness meter. the ledger asks one thing: did you choose a kept thing over a free one. every
option has a price that is said out loud, and the regent's reading of your choices is his own.

## on briar

written as taken, as decided. the canticle's version (she dies of the severing wound and is brought back) hits
harder on the night and makes the envoy's promise larger. taken is the better fit for this story: it lets him send a
receipt, lets him say "she is safe" and be telling the truth, and turns the false fox into an offer instead of a
trick. if dom wants the death, three scenes change (`night.bridge`, `night.receipt`, `hell.pattern`) and nothing else.

## what was borrowed and what was not

the shapes this could be mistaken for, and where it differs: the keeper who hands you his office (he does not; in the
kept ending he keeps you), the companion killed to motivate you (she is taken, safe, and offered back), the true name
that frees a monster (the name frees nothing; reading the forty-one aloud, and one apology a hundred years late, do).
